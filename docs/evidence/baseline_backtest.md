# Baseline backtest: how often the real five-year total fell inside the band

Generated by `hre validate --baseline` on 2026-11-03 from the Census Building Permits Survey state annual files 1980-2025 (units authorised, estimates with imputation; https://www2.census.gov/econ/bps/State/), 51 jurisdictions (50 states and DC). Code: `src/hre/validation/backtest_baseline.py`.

**How to read this.** A forecast *origin* is the last year of data a forecast uses; the forecast covers the next five years. For every state and origin we compute the baseline the way the tool does today and compare it with what was actually permitted. The bands below are assumption sets fitted to history. "Inside in 80% of past periods" is a count of past periods, not a probability that the future lands inside.

## 5. Which band is the headline, and why both are shown

The tool's headline band is rule A. Rule B is shown beside it in every results file (`baseline.alternative_band`), in its own section, computed exactly as tested here: the last five years' total stretched by the state's own slowest and fastest five years of the past 20 (decision 66). Rule A makes one checkable promise (inside in 80% of past periods) that held out of sample and allows for a shock the size of 2008. Rule B is narrower and follows each state's own history, but it promises no fixed coverage and missed most states after 2005. Showing both lets a reader see the trade-off between a wide band that rarely misses and a tight band that sometimes does.

## 6. Plain-language reading

- In 80% of past five-year periods from origins 1990-2015, the actual statewide permit total fell between 0.54 and 1.59 times the 3-year-mean baseline (80% by construction: that is how the band was fitted).
- On origins 2016-2020, which the fit never saw, 98% of actual totals fell inside (251 of 255); 3 were above the band and 1 below.
- Adam's rule put 71% of his 255 runs inside; its band is set by each state's own swings, so it is narrow in steady states and wide in boom-and-bust states.
- Neither band is a probability. Both are summaries of how far five-year building has strayed from a simple continuation in the past; a recession or a boom outside that history would fall outside.
- The band is fitted on state totals and applied to counties as well; a single county's error is larger, and the results file says so.
